## Confirmed Finding

Investigation CD6 found that evaluation activity is highly concentrated in a small number of clinicians across all wards rather than within individual wards. Loss or sustained absence of a high-volume clinician therefore affects the entire facility simultaneously, not a single service.

Current concentration levels and the highest-volume clinician should always come from the snapshot.

---

## Supporting Evidence

The investigation showed that when a high-volume clinician left the service, workload redistributed rapidly to the remaining clinicians without a formal redistribution process — increasing concentration risk rather than resolving it.

Private wards were more vulnerable than general wards because they relied on fewer evaluating clinicians. Private ward admission volumes track the presence of specific high-volume clinicians more directly than general wards do.

---

## Operational Implications

- Concentration risk is facility-wide rather than ward-specific.
- Sustained increases in one clinician's workload may indicate redistribution after a staffing change rather than increased demand.
- Redistribution should be attempted before considering additional staffing.
- A private ward volume drop is more likely a clinician presence signal than a demand signal — check the snapshot's entity-level workload data first.
- Current workload, concentration share, and the highest-volume clinician come from the snapshot, not this file.
